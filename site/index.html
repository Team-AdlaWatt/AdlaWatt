Why it exists

Built for the space between outages.

A backup battery is only useful when you know what it can carry. AdlaWatt makes stored solar energy visible, understandable, and easier to plan around.

System flow

From sunlight to a decision.

Power moves through the hardware. Data moves through the ESP32, Supabase, and the mobile app.

  1. 01

    Harvest

    A solar panel charges the battery through a charge controller.

  2. 02

    Store

    Two ZENOVA 30Ah batteries are connected in parallel for a 12 V, 60 Ah bank.

  3. 03

    Convert

    An inverter supplies the built-in AC outlet for compatible appliances.

  4. 04

    Sense

    An ESP32-S3 reads solar, load, voltage, and temperature sensors.

  5. 05

    Guide

    The app shows live status and labels appliances OK to use or not advisable.

Current status: the mobile app is integrated with Supabase; the ESP32 hardware feed is being wired up end to end.

What the app does

One system, six useful views.

The app turns raw sensor readings into the information a household actually needs during an outage.

Capability ledger 06 entries

01 · Live data

Real-time energy monitoring

Battery status, voltage, energy, current load, depth of discharge, solar input, solar voltage and current, temperatures, and device status.

02 · Guidance

Appliance recommendations

Each appliance is labeled OK to use or not advisable based on remaining energy, voltage, and power draw.

03 · Weather

Weather information

Current weather information for the system location, shown alongside the live energy readings.

04 · Alerts

Notifications

Push notifications for critical battery, temperature, solar, load, and device-status alerts.

05 · Reports

Historical data and reports

Historical charts and reports for selected date ranges and frequencies.

06 · Activity

Activity logs

A history of system events, kept separate from notifications.

Questions

AdlaWatt, explained.

The short answers to the questions people ask most about the system, the app, and the project.

06 questions · one system

What is AdlaWatt?

AdlaWatt is an IoT-based transportable off-grid solar backup power system with an ESP32 monitor and a mobile app for real-time energy monitoring, appliance recommendations, notifications, and outage planning.

How does AdlaWatt monitor solar and battery data?

An ESP32-S3 reads voltage, current, and temperature sensors, sends the readings to Supabase, and the AdlaWatt mobile app displays live battery, solar, load, and system-health data.

What appliances can AdlaWatt power?

AdlaWatt works with compatible household appliances up to 600 W total through its built-in AC outlet. High-power appliances such as air-conditioners, washing machines, and electric stoves are excluded. The app compares remaining energy, battery voltage, and appliance power draw to label each item OK to use or not advisable.

Is AdlaWatt available for purchase?

No. AdlaWatt is an academic capstone prototype in development and is not a commercial product.

Is the AdlaWatt source code open source?

The repository is publicly viewable, but no open-source license is specified. Reuse, redistribution, or modification requires permission from the authors.

Where was AdlaWatt built?

AdlaWatt was developed by the AdlaWatt Research and Development Team at Northern Bukidnon State College in Bukidnon, Philippines. The prototype was evaluated in Sto. Niño, Manolo Fortich, Bukidnon.
